How to annotate (quick guide)
  1. Videos are usually shared with you as a folder (one batch of about 100 files). Press Load videos folder once and pick that folder: the videos then play directly from your computer — nothing is uploaded. Without the folder the contact sheet (timestamped frames) is shown.
  2. Open a sample from the list. Watch the video (or the contact sheet when no video is available) and use the phase timeline and evidence chips to jump to the relevant moments.
  3. Read the three AI-generated questions and their gold answers. For each question: judge whether the answer is correct, rate clinical relevance, difficulty, agentic/multi-step nature and clarity (1–5), and fix the wording of the question or answer if needed.
  4. Select the best 1–2 question(s) for the benchmark (tick Select for benchmark). Prefer questions that need several reasoning steps across the video and that a clinician can verify.
  5. Add sample-level flags (video quality, wrong metadata, not suitable, duplicate) and comments where relevant, then press Mark complete.
  6. Everything is saved automatically in this browser. When you finish a session press Submit (or Export JSON and send us the file).

How to annotate

You are reviewing short ophthalmic-surgery videos together with three questions and gold answers written by an AI model. The goal is a benchmark of tough, agentic questions: ones that need several reasoning steps across the video (seeking, counting, measuring, ordering phases, applying clinical judgment) and whose answers a clinician can verify from the video and its labels.

Videos folder (offline batches)

The videos are shared with you as a folder per batch (batch_001, …). On the list page press Load videos folder and choose that folder; the browser reads the files from your disk when you open a sample and never uploads them. Chrome/Edge remember the folder between visits (press Reconnect when asked); other browsers ask you to select it again after a reload. Use the Batch filter to work through one batch at a time.

Per sample

  1. Watch the video. Click a timeline segment or an evidence chip to jump the video to that moment. If the video does not load, the contact sheet (sampled frames with timestamps) is shown instead.
  2. Check the metadata panel (procedure, phases, surgeon experience, skill scores, flags…). If it is wrong, tick the Metadata wrong flag.
  3. For each of the three questions fill in the controls below. Then tick Select for benchmark for the 1–2 best question(s) and press Mark complete.

Question controls

Select for benchmark
Keep this question (with your edits) in the final benchmark. At least 1, at most 2 per sample. Prefer verifiable, multi-step, clinically meaningful questions.
Answer correctness
Correct — the gold answer is right; Partially correct — right in substance but imprecise or incomplete; Incorrect — wrong; Cannot verify — the video/labels do not allow you to decide.
Clinical relevance 1–5
1 = trivia, 5 = a question a surgical trainer would genuinely ask.
Difficulty 1–5
1 = answerable from one glance or from the metadata alone, 5 = needs careful review of several parts of the video.
Agentic / multi-step 1–5
1 = a single observation, 5 = requires planning several actions (seek, count, measure, compare segments, look up knowledge) and verification.
Clarity 1–5
1 = ambiguous or confusing wording, 5 = unambiguous with exactly one defensible answer.
Question / Answer text
Edit freely to fix wording, precision or errors. Your version is what goes into the benchmark; an edited marker appears and Reset restores the original.
Comment
Anything the study team should know about this question.

Sample flags

  • Video quality issue — blur, missing segments, wrong video, unusable preview.
  • Metadata wrong — procedure, phase labels or other metadata do not match the video.
  • Not suitable — the video should not be in the benchmark (e.g. not surgical, identifiable patient, too short to ask anything).
  • Duplicate — you have seen this same video under another sample id.

You can still mark a flagged sample complete; if nothing is worth selecting, tick Not suitable, select the least-bad question and explain in the comment.

Saving, exporting and submitting

  • Every change is saved immediately in this browser (local storage), under your annotator ID. Clearing site data or switching browsers loses unsaved work, so Submit or Export regularly.
  • Export JSON downloads all your annotations; Import JSON merges a file exported earlier (newer entries win), e.g. to continue on another computer.
  • Submit sends your annotations to the study server (when configured). It is safe to submit several times; the latest version of each sample is kept.
